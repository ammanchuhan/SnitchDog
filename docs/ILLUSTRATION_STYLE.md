# Accountable — illustration style

Accountable has its own look, separate from ammanchuhan.com: **Ember**, a small flame, in a
**cut-paper and felt-tip marker** style. **Ember is the AI coach**: the voice of the morning
messages, the Coach tab and the note to the witness. Ember is named after the app's accent colour and carries the
emotion the interface can't: bright and proud when you keep your word, small and worried when a week
slips, asleep when nothing is due. Every image is generated by Amman from the prompts below; I
work out the style and the prompts, and wire the results into the app.

**Prompts are assembled from the blocks below, word for word.** Paraphrasing a block is how a
character drifts between images.

## Workflow

1. **Generate the model sheet first** (prompt 0). Regenerate until Ember looks right: this sheet
   defines the character for everything else.
2. Save it as `accountable-ember-sheet.png`, and **upload it as the reference image for every other
   prompt.** Every pose prompt says "exactly as in the reference sheet" and means this file.
3. Generate each pose at **1024×1024**. Save to Downloads as `accountable-ember-<name>.png`.
4. I key out the magenta with `scripts/key-illustration.py`, trim, and export transparent PNGs at
   @2x/@3x into `assets/illustrations/`.

## Why magenta, and no paper patch

Each image is cut out and placed straight onto the app's background, so it's generated on flat
magenta: a colour that appears nowhere in the palette, so it can be keyed out cleanly
(`scripts/key-illustration.py`). The first model sheet had a torn paper-white patch behind each
pose, meant to keep Ember's black marker lines visible on the dark theme. It was dropped: on the
light theme it blends into the background and reads as a smudge, and in the conversational
sign-up it repeats as clutter. On the dark theme Ember sits in a soft ember-coloured glow
instead, which suits a flame.

## The blocks

1. **Style** (first): `A handmade cut-paper illustration with felt-tip marker details: every shape is cut from matte coloured paper with slightly uneven scissor edges and visible paper fibre texture, layers cast small soft paper shadows on each other, and faces, hands and fine details are drawn in loose black felt-tip marker, printed slightly off-register.`
2. **Character**: `The character is Ember, exactly as in the reference sheet: a small, plump teardrop-shaped flame about the size of a coffee mug, cut from ember orange #E8552F paper with a smaller tangerine #FF8A5C inner flame layered on top, a soft three-point flickering tip, two dot eyes and a small curved mouth drawn in black felt-tip marker, and short stubby marker-line arms and legs with round mitten hands.`
3. **Scene** and **Composition**: written fresh per image.
4. **Palette**: `Palette limited to: ember orange #E8552F, tangerine #FF8A5C, deep green #157F55, butter yellow #F6C945, soft sky blue #9CC3E6, warm paper white #FBFAF8, and black #16150F for marker lines only.`
5. **Background**: `Place the scene directly on a perfectly flat, solid magenta #FF00FF background with no gradient, no paper patch, no floor and no shadow on the background, with generous padding on every side, so the magenta can be cleanly removed. Nothing in the scene is magenta.`
6. **No text**: `No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos anywhere.` plus the image-specific ban.
7. **Closer** (last): `Warm picture-book charm with grown-up restraint. Not 3D, not glossy, not flat vector art.`

The palette comes from the app's own theme (`src/theme.ts`): ember and tangerine for Ember and
anything urgent, deep green for "done", on warm paper white.

## Ember's rules

- Ember never judges the number on the scale. It reacts to *showing up*, the same rule as the
  coach (spec §8.2).
- Worried, never angry or disappointed. The witness is the consequence; Ember is on your side.
- Size can carry meaning: tiny for "easing in", huge for "all in".

## Everything to generate: Ember as parts

Ember is **assembled in code from parts**, not generated pose by pose. Image models drift: the
same character never comes back quite the same twice. So each part is generated once, and every
pose (happy, proud, worried, waving, lifting a dumbbell) is the same body with different eyes,
mouth, arms and legs placed on it. That keeps Ember identical everywhere, makes blinking a swap of
the eyes, and makes the wave smooth, because the arm rotates at the shoulder rather than jumping
between two drawings.

Five images. **Upload `accountable-ember-sheet.png` (the model sheet) as the reference for every
one.** Highest resolution the tool offers. File names don't matter; they're recognised on sight.

| # | Part | Aspect | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | The body, no face or limbs | 3:4 | Every pose. Generate this at the highest resolution; it's shown largest. |
| P2 | Eyes and mouths | 4:3 | Expressions and blinking. |
| P3 | Arms and legs | 4:3 | Poses; the wave rotates the raised arm. |
| P4 | Props: scale, phone, dumbbell, paper plane, alarm clock, sparks | 4:3 | Scenes on Home, the weigh-in and the invite. |
| P5 | The witness: a friend with a phone | 3:4 | The witness screen, with Ember on their shoulder. |

Parts on a sheet are cut apart, keyed and scaled to match the body, then placed from anchor
points (where the eyes sit, where each shoulder and hip is) recorded once in the rig.
